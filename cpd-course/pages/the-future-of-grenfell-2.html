The Future of Grenfell (2)

Week 3

Answer these reflection questions on the future of Grenfell.
Question

Why has criminal justice taken so long to materialise for the Grenfell community, and what tensions exist between thoroughness of investigation and the human need for timely accountability?

Write a reflection

Your notes stay in this page and are not submitted or saved.

The wait for criminal justice

The criminal investigation has been exceptionally complex, involving 700 organisations, 165 million electronic files, and potential charges ranging from corporate manslaughter to fraud. Police needed to wait for the Inquiry’s Phase 2 report (published September 2024) before completing their files for the CPS. While thoroughness increases the chance of successful prosecution, the near-decade wait has caused immense distress to bereaved families, encapsulated in the phrase “justice delayed is justice denied”. This tension raises the question of whether a legal system can meet the emotional and moral needs of victims even when it is working as designed.

Question

What does the decision to demolish Grenfell Tower, rather than preserve it, reveal about the challenges of memorialising a tragedy while also supporting a living community?

Write a reflection

Your notes stay in this page and are not submitted or saved.

Memorialisation and a living community

The demolition decision reflects a difficult balance. Engineering reports confirmed the tower was deteriorating and posed safety risks, making preservation impractical. For some, the tower was a sacred connection to lost loved ones; for others, it was a source of daily trauma. The choice to demolish and replace it with a community-designed memorial acknowledges that memorialisation must serve the living community by offering a space for peace, reflection and remembrance, rather than preserving a painful ruin. It also shows how memorials must evolve through genuine community participation, not top-down decisions.
